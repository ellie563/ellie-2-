01 / Research Design · Positivist Study

Elderly-care spending and youth-program funding in South Korean municipalities

PLS 210 · Presentation #1

Question
02

Research Question

"To what extent did rising municipal spending on elderly care reduce local government funding for youth programs in South Korea between 2015 and 2023?"

EmpiricalAsks what did happen to spending, not what should happen — answerable with budget data, not opinion.
Population & contextMunicipal governments in South Korea, not governments broadly.
Time window2015–2023: the earliest years South Korea provides standardized, comparable integrated financial statements across all local governments (Ministry of Public Administration and Security), making this the first period a consistent municipal panel is possible.
AnswerableMunicipal-level spending data for both categories exist in Local Finance 365 across this window.
Where This Sits
03

Epistemology & Positioning

StancePositivist
JustificationMunicipal budgets are observable, measurable facts. "To what extent" asks for the size of an effect, which requires measurement across many cases, not interpretation of meaning.
DesignStatistical, large-N panel across municipalities — not a small-N comparative case study.
What this rules outAn interpretivist approach would ask how officials experience or justify these trade-offs. That is a different question, set aside here.
What It Adds
04

Contribution

  • The Korean fiscal literature explains how local budgets are divided in general — through decentralization (Kwon 2003; Park 2022), partisanship and transfers (Ha & Lee 2022), and citizen participation (Lee & Min 2023).
  • None of it opens up the welfare category itself to test whether growth in one age-targeted line squeezes another.
  • This project tests one specific, unexamined trade-off inside that literature: whether rising elderly-care spending came at the direct expense of youth-program funding, during a period of accelerating ageing.
What We Already Know
05

Literature Review

1. Decentralization reshapes composition, not just size

Kwon (2003): as Korea decentralized, local governments gained real spending discretion while the central share shrank — decentralization changes who decides, not only how much.
Park (2022): revenue-side decentralization specifically reduces welfare's share of local budgets and shifts spending from current to capital accounts.

2. Political and institutional levers decide which categories move

Ha & Lee (2022): politically aligned regions receive larger transfers, and those transfers raise social-welfare and education spending; progressive mayors spend significantly more on both than conservative mayors.
Lee & Min (2023): participatory budgeting shifts spending toward welfare, health, and transport and away from long-term development.

Where they disagree

Park finds decentralization shrinks welfare's share; Ha & Lee and Lee & Min find specific channels — subsidies, participation, progressive leadership — that grow it. Welfare's fate depends on which lever is pulled, not on decentralization as one thing.

The gap

Every study here works at the level of broad categories (welfare vs. development, welfare vs. administration). None tests whether growth in one welfare line — elderly care — comes at the expense of another — youth programs.

Theory
06

Theory and Hypotheses

Mechanism: Elderly-care spending is largely non-discretionary — tied to statutory welfare obligations, often co-financed with the central government. Youth-program spending is comparatively discretionary. Municipal budgets are fixed in a given year. When a non-discretionary category grows faster than the total budget, the squeeze falls on flexible lines — youth programs among them.

H1: Municipalities with a larger rise in elderly-care spending, 2015–2023, saw a larger fall in youth-program funding.
H0: Changes in elderly-care spending are unrelated to changes in youth-program funding.
Independent variableChange in elderly-care spending, 2015–2023 (share of municipal budget)
Dependent variableChange in youth-program funding over the same period
DataPanel of municipalities, Local Finance 365 (Ministry of the Interior and Safety)
ControlsRevenue decentralization (Park 2022), political ideology of mayor (Ha & Lee 2022), presence of participatory budgeting (Lee & Min 2023), total budget size
